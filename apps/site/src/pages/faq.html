Questions

What is Boss Man?

A place to run teams of AI agents organised as companies. Each company has a CEO and agents with roles. They work tickets on a board, review each other's work and bring decisions to you.

What does it cost?

Boss Man itself is free and open source under the Apache-2.0 licence. You pay for your own machine and your own AI accounts. Boss Man tracks that spend and acts on the limits you set.

Which AI does it use?

Your own. Agents run Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode, so you can use a Claude plan, an Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter key, or a mix. Model lists come from the agents themselves, so they stay current.

Is my code safe?

Boss Man runs on your machine and your network. Each company has its own container and secrets, encrypted with a key only you hold. Your code and keys stay on your machine. Back up the master key: without it, stored secrets can't be read.

Can agents ship things without me?

Only as far as you let them. Work is reviewed before it merges, and high-risk work needs your sign-off. Boss Man asks you before it deploys a new version of itself. Budgets and a kill switch stop runaway spending.

Can my team use it too?

Not yet. This version has one owner. Several human users are not planned for the first version.

Is there a hosted version?

No. Boss Man is self-hosted only. That's a deliberate choice: your agents work where your code and keys already are.

How is it different from Paperclip?

Boss Man was inspired by Paperclip. It is written from scratch and doesn't depend on it. It adds companies that hire each other, a board you can shape, one decision queue, escalation of stuck work, real budgets and quota tracking, and goals with a roadmap. You can move a company over from Paperclip.

Is it ready to use?

It's alpha. Boss Man's own agents build Boss Man, but expect rough edges. Use it for your own projects, and keep it off the open internet.
