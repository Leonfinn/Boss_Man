Boss Man runs AI agents from a ticket board on your server

Turn feedback into a ranked roadmap, and let AI agents plan, write and review the work on the board. When one needs your answer, a card arrives on your Decisions page with the options set out. Code is optional: agents can research a market or draft a client report in your wiki, or change software in your git repository.

Decisions is the one page where agents ask you things

Questions from every company you run arrive on this page, and the sidebar shows how many are waiting. If you do not answer, only the work that depends on that card waits.

  1. ?

    The question in plain words

    Each card is linked to the ticket it holds up, so you can read the background.

  2. ✓

    What the agent tried first

    The agent must say what it tried and why it needs you. Boss Man refuses a card that leaves this vague.

  3. ★

    Its recommendation

    Every question names the option the agent recommends.

  4. →

    Your answer

    Choose an option, write your own answer, or ask the CEO for advice. The ticket it held up then carries on.

Away from your desk? With the ntfy add-on, urgent cards reach your phone, and you can answer them in your phone's browser.

Feedback, features and a roadmap, next to the work

Log what people ask for, link it to features, and Boss Man ranks them by demand and effort. Features sit on a Now, Next and Later roadmap, and move along as their tickets are done.

The Roadmap page, and the feedback portal your users see. Real screenshots, made-up data.

  1. Feedback

    Every request in one place

    Paste in feedback, or let agents log what they hear. Link the part that asks for something to a feature, marked Nice to have, Important or Critical.

  2. Priority

    A score you can follow

    Each feature is scored on demand and on your own criteria, then divided by effort. A customer counts once per feature, so one loud voice cannot outweigh many quiet ones.

  3. Roadmap

    A roadmap tied to the tickets

    Put features in Now, Next or Later, under an objective or a dated milestone. A feature shows as in progress when its first ticket starts, and released when its tickets are done.

  4. Public roadmap

    A page for your users

    The feedback portal shows the features you choose as Under consideration, Planned or Released. Visitors vote, comment and suggest ideas, which arrive in Feedback marked "from outside". Anyone with its secret link can open it. You can ask visitors to confirm their posts by email.

Boss Man's own roadmap is on one of these portals. See what's planned next, vote for what matters to you, or suggest something new.

Give it five minutes or an afternoon

The agents keep working while you're away, and anything that needs you waits in one place. However much time you have today, you spend it where it counts.

  1. Five minutes

    Answer what's waiting

    Open Decisions and clear the cards, on your phone if you like. Each says what the agent tried and which answer it recommends, so most take one tap.

  2. An hour

    Check the finished work

    Open what's in Your review, read what changed and try it. Approve it and Boss Man runs your checks and merges it, or send it back with a note.

  3. An afternoon

    Plan what comes next

    Read the new feedback, move features (what your users get) along the roadmap and write the next tasks that build them. Or ask the CEO to plan something bigger and split it into tasks. See features, tasks and sub-tasks.

While you're away

  • Agents pick up tickets that are Ready and work on their own branches.
  • They review each other's changes before anything merges.
  • A stuck ticket says what it is waiting for. If you hire a Scrum Master, it chases the ticket.
  • Anything that needs you waits in Decisions, and can reach your phone.

Reviews, permissions, models and the rest

Reviews that match the risk
You choose who reviews work at each risk level. Low-risk work can need only one reviewer, such as a senior developer. High-risk work can also get security, infrastructure and adversarial reviews. By default you approve its plan first, and sign it off before it merges.
Specs that get it right first time
Describe what you want in a few lines. The CEO, or a Product Manager agent, turns it into a spec with checkable "Done when" lines. Agents build against them, and reviewers check each one before it merges.
Permissions
Agents ask you before they push, publish or delete data. Tools Boss Man does not recognise go to the CEO first, then to you.
Spending caps
Set a cost cap per task, and monthly spending limits per project, agent or company. Monthly limits count pay-as-you-go spend.
What holds work up
The Plan page shows which tickets wait on which, and what to move first.
Your choice of models
Agents run Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode. Use a Claude or ChatGPT plan, or an API key from Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Ollama Cloud or Synthetic.
Add-ons when you want them
Phone notifications with ntfy, HTTPS from anywhere with Tailscale, Penpot for designer agents, and a browser for agents that test your site.
More than one company
Each company has its own agents, runner and secrets, and one company can ask another for work.

Install it on a Linux machine with Docker

Download two files from a release and run three Docker commands: Boss Man comes as ready-made images, so nothing is built on your machine. Two guides take you from there to your first task, with a check after each step: Install Boss Man (steps 1 to 6) and Your first company and task (steps 7 to 12).

  • A 64-bit Linux machine (x86-64 or arm64) with Docker Engine and Docker Compose v2. A home server, NAS or Proxmox VM works.
  • A Claude or ChatGPT plan, or an API key from Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Ollama Cloud or Synthetic. Which Claude account to choose.
  • Only if your agents will work on code: a GitHub or Gitea account.
  • To be happy pasting commands into a terminal.

What it costs: Boss Man itself is free. You pay for your own Claude plan, or for what you use on an API key. See What agents cost.

Why Linux?

Boss Man runs on a 64-bit Linux machine with Docker. Two safety features use the Linux kernel: the network guard keeps agents off your home network, and the agent fence keeps each agent in its own folder. Settings shows whether each one is working on your machine.

Mac and Windows: Docker Desktop 4.60 or newer is a preview. It is not yet proven on a real machine, so for now use a Linux machine.
